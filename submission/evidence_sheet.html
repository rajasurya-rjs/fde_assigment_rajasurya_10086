Can TLC trust its wheelchair-accessible 10-minute numbers?

ProblemTLC cannot verify monthly whether Uber and Lyft serve wheelchair users on time: the evidence is split across 21 M-row trip files and control reports, and timestamp errors inflate compliance.
→
KPIWAV 10-min service rate: share of wheelchair-accessible (WAV) requests with the vehicle on scene < 600 s after the request, per dispatcher-month. Target ≥ 90% (35 RCNY §59B-17(f)(3)).
→
Decision supportedTLC accessibility compliance: open a compliance review or a data-correction request; where and when to target WAV supply; which months are safe to publish.
Headline. Uber meets the standard (94.3% across Apr/May/Jul 2026): proven in April, an estimate in May/July, where the bounds straddle 90%. The margin is shrinking (95.8 → 94.8 → 92.2%). Lyft's compliance cannot be determined: 58–61% of its WAV records have the vehicle arriving before the request (raw median −6.4 min). Unvalidated, Lyft looks compliant (92.7%). Silently filtered, it looks non-compliant (81.0%). The pipeline withholds the number and flags a data-correction request. June 2026 was refused: the file is 2.14% short of TLC's own monthly report.

1Problem worth solving

  • A regulated, enforceable standard (notice + 30 days to comply) for a vulnerable rider group, measured on data the dispatchers submit themselves.
  • TLC's FY23 accessibility report, recommendation #1: "Add FHV WAV service metrics to the TLC Dashboard." Today it is an annual PDF.

2Why it matters (and why this track)

  • The 2025 rule made Uber/Lyft report on-scene time "so that TLC can better compare WAV and non-WAV wait times". M3/M4 do exactly that.
  • Only High-Volume FHV records carry request and on-scene times. Users: TLC compliance (primary), the TLC data team, Uber/Lyft WAV ops.

4Workflow and data model

Entities: dispatcher (licensee → base B03404 Uber / B03406 Lyft) · trip · pickup zone → borough · vehicle only via wav_match_flag (no ID). Intervention: WAV dispatch duty, TLC notice. Outcome: on scene < 10 min · rider wait · gap vs non-WAV.
Model (DuckDB): fct_wav_trip PK (month, source_row_id) = 1 WAV-requested trip, quarantined rows kept with reasons · ref_licensee / ref_zone PKs · agg_segment_month PK (month, level, licensee, WAV, borough, band) over all 21 M trips → views M1–M5. Joins are safe: each is a LEFT JOIN onto a reference PK (many → 1), so it cannot drop or multiply trips; unmatched keys fail REF01/02. Tested: dispatcher totals = fact rows = Σ cells.

10Known · Unknown · Assumption · Limitation

Known

Rule: ≥ 90% under 10 min; passenger cancels within 10 min excluded. TLC measures to vehicle arrival. Apr/May reconcile exactly; the June file is −2.14%. 0 duplicates.

Unknown

Unserved or cancelled WAV requests. The cause of Lyft's defect. Whether pre-booked rides fall under the rule. Whether June's gap is Lyft (inferred).

Assumption

Population = completed trips with wav_request_flag=Y; dispatcher = licensee; "under" = < 600 s; month = pickup month; an "estimate" treats quarantined rows as out of scope (Uber: pre-booked signature).

Limitation

Rates are upper bounds (completed trips only). Controls prove the file was received, not that every trip was submitted. No vehicle IDs. 3 of 7 months of 2026 published.

11FDE judgement call (demo): withhold Lyft's KPI instead of reporting either available number

A · Naive rate92.7% → "compliant". Counts impossible negative times as on-time.
B · Drop bad rows81.0% → "non-compliant", notice issued. Survivors are a biased slice if the request time is stamped late.
C · ImputeFabricates the regulated quantity itself.
D · Withhold + bounds + data request ✔NOT MEASURABLE (31–93%); names the field, base, record path and size.

Systematic, not noise: 58–61% in 3 months; one base; Lyft's WAV rows are its only rows with originating_base_num set (separate record path); no pre-booking signature; even "valid" rows are slower than every peer (P90 12.5 vs 8.5 min). One rule for all: bounds on one side of 90% → proven (Uber, Apr); straddling, with ≥ 90% clean → estimate (Uber, May/Jul; the line is a stated policy equal to the standard's tolerance); otherwise withheld (Lyft: bounds 31–93%, 40% clean).

Decisions: Lyft → data-correction request · Uber → compliant; confirming the pre-booked rides would make May/Jul proven · supply → Queens 20–24 (every month) + July's Queens/Bronx night windows · TLC → add a pre-booked flag and unserved-request records · June 2026 → hold.
